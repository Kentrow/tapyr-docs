Glossary¶

The words Tapyr uses in its configuration, its CLI, its web UI and its metrics, in alphabetical order. Where the web UI shows a term under another label, the entry says so. Concepts explains how the pieces fit together.

Account¶

An instance of a provider: its secrets, its params (an organization, a base URL) and its labels, added to every sample it produces. You write one Account document per set of credentials. See Accounts and targets.

Budget¶

What a quota leaves to Tapyr until its reset: its maximum, times the budget share, minus what is already spent and the retry reserve. The planner fits the jobs into it. In the web UI: Available, on the Plan page.

Budget share¶

The part of every quota an account may spend, budget.share on the account (0.5 leaves half for other tools using the same credentials). Default: the whole quota.

Burst¶

The number of requests a rate limit lets through at once after a quiet period, before it falls back to its rate: the size of its token bucket.

Circuit breaker¶

Protects an account (or a target) that keeps failing: after 5 consecutive transient failures it opens, and Tapyr stops calling, so no quota is spent. After a cooldown (2 minutes, doubling up to 30) it is half-open: the next run is a probe, which closes it on success and opens it again on failure. See Failures.

Concurrency limit¶

A limit on the requests in flight at the same time (kind: concurrency): a request waits for a free slot. In the web UI: "requests at once".

Credential¶

A set of secret values. A limit scoped by credential is shared by the accounts that use the same secrets, even under different names. Tapyr keys it by a salted hash and never shows the secret; the status API and the web UI name the accounts that share it.

Desired interval¶

How often you want a job to run: interval on the job, else the defaultInterval of its template. The planner may stretch it into a longer effective interval.

Discovery¶

Listing the items of a hierarchical API (organizations, then their networks, then their devices) so that the jobs below run once per item. An inventory job discovers, a fan-out job runs on what it found. See Discovery.

Effective interval¶

The interval a job actually runs at: its desired interval, or longer when the planner stretches it to fit a quota. The Jobs page shows both when they differ.

Endpoint¶

The path of a request with its variables left as placeholders, such as /organizations/{organizationId}/networks. Limits can be scoped by endpoint, and the self-metrics use it rather than the real URL, so that they never carry identifiers.

External usage¶

The calls of other clients of the same quota (the vendor UI, scripts, other integrations): what the provider counts minus what Tapyr made. Known only when the API sends quota headers. The planner expects it to go on at the same pace until the reset. In the web UI: Used by others, on the Budgets page; self-metric tapyr_budget_external_usage.

Fan-out¶

A job whose template has forEach: it runs once per item listed by its parent, as many instances. "The fan-out of a job" is also the number of its instances.

Implicit job¶

The parent of a fan-out that no job of the account declares: Tapyr runs it for you, named <account>.<template> (campus.meraki-organizations), at priority low. You only declare the jobs whose metrics you want. The web UI names it by what it lists and for whom ("Networks, listed for network-devices"), with its technical name below, and tapyr plan lists the implicit jobs after the quotas.

Infeasible plan¶

A plan in which the critical jobs alone spend more than a quota allows: they are never stretched, so nothing can make them fit. tapyr plan exits with code 3 and says what to change; tapyr run starts anyway, with a warning. In the web UI: does not fit.

Instance¶

Two meanings, told apart by their context:

  • A job instance is one copy of a fan-out job, for one item of its parent: the devices job of one network. The Instances column of the Jobs and Plan pages counts them, and the page of a job lists them.
  • A Tapyr instance is one running Tapyr process. A limit scoped by instance is shared by every account of the process: it stands for the limits a provider counts per source IP. The web UI and tapyr plan call this scope "the whole Tapyr process".

Inventory¶

A job whose template lists items for others (role: inventory, or both when it also produces metrics). Its last answer is kept in the state store, so that a restart does not wait for discovery.

Job¶

What to collect for an account: a template of its provider (or an inline request), at an interval and a priority, towards every output or some of them. See Jobs.

Job template¶

A request of a provider package and its mapping to metrics, which jobs name with template. tapyr provider show <name> lists the templates of a package, with their interval and metrics.

Limit¶

A rule of a provider API that Tapyr keeps to: a rate limit, a concurrency limit or a quota, declared once in the provider package with its scope and the source of its value. The Budgets page shows every limit with its current state.

Not collected¶

A job whose metrics no output keeps (every output drops them by name): it makes no API call and spends no quota, and its quota goes to the other jobs. tapyr validate and tapyr plan warn about it. See Sending less to an output.

Output¶

Where the samples go: prometheus (served on /metrics for a scrape), remote_write or otlp. Each push output has its own queue, so a slow backend never holds back the others. See Outputs.

Pages per run¶

The requests a run makes when the answer comes in several pages: the median of the last runs, else the estimate of the template, else 1. The planner multiplies it by the instances and the runs per window.

Penalty¶

After a 429 Too Many Requests, the scope of the limit waits for the Retry-After of the response (2 seconds without one), longer on repeated ones, up to 10 minutes. The requests of that scope wait or are refused in the meantime.

Plan¶

The intervals the planner gives the jobs so that they fit every quota until its reset, with the jobs it slowed down and why. tapyr plan computes it offline, before you deploy; the Plan page and GET /api/v1/plan show the plan applied. See Planner.

Planner¶

The part of Tapyr that projects what the jobs will spend in every quota window and stretches their intervals when it does not fit. It plans again every 15 minutes, after a reload or a discovery, and at once when a quota would run out before its reset, for example because of the external usage.

Priority¶

Who slows down first when a quota is tight: low first, then normal (the default), then high; critical jobs are never slowed down. Requests waiting on the same limit also go in priority order.

Processing¶

Rules on an output (spec.processing) that drop metrics or labels before they reach it, written like Prometheus metric_relabel_configs. A job none of whose metrics reaches any output is not collected.

Projected¶

What the jobs will spend until the reset of a quota at their planned intervals, on the Plan page. Use is the projected spend against the budget.

Provider¶

A vendor API as Tapyr knows it: base URL, authentication, pagination, errors and limits, in a Provider document.

Provider package¶

A directory that describes one provider: the Provider document, its job templates, a simulator, a Grafana dashboard and a README, in YAML and CEL. The built-in packages come with the binary; settings.providerPaths adds your own. See Providers.

Quota¶

A number of calls per window (kind: quota): per minute, hour, day, week or month, or per rolling period. Once it is spent, Tapyr makes no more calls in its scope until the reset. In the web UI: "calls per day", "calls per 1h, rolling".

Quota headers¶

Response headers in which an API announces its quota: its maximum, what is left, when it resets. When a provider declares them, Tapyr follows them: it learns the real maximum, measures the external usage and never spends more than the API says is left. In the web UI: "reported by the API", next to Remaining on the Budgets page.

Rate limit¶

A limit on requests per second or per minute (kind: rate), as a token bucket: a request waits for a token, and a burst can go at once.

Replan¶

The planner computing the plan again from what is left in each window, so that a morning of retries or another client tightens the afternoon instead of exhausting the quota.

Retry reserve¶

The part of every quota kept for retries and for critical jobs, 10% by default (settings.budget.retryReserve): the planner leaves it out, so that a retry never finds the quota empty. In the web UI: For retries on the Plan page, Retry reserve on the Budgets page.

Role¶

What a template is for: metrics (the default) produces samples, inventory lists items for other templates, both does both.

Run¶

One execution of a job instance: its requests, pages and retries, and the samples it produced. Its result is one of:

Result Meaning
success every request succeeded and the samples were sent
partial some pages failed and the template accepts partial data (extract.partial)
failed a request or the mapping failed; the series stay until stale
skipped_budget no quota left, or the scope is under penalty
skipped_circuit the circuit breaker of the account is open
skipped_overlap the previous run of the instance was still going

A job with no run yet is waiting: for its first run, or for its parent to list its items.

Sample¶

One value of one series at one time. A sample carries the time the API gives when the mapping reads it, else the time of the collection.

Scope¶

What a limit is counted per: the whole Tapyr instance (instance), every account of the provider together (provider), each account, each target, each credential, each endpoint, or each value of a request variable (var:organizationId). A limit scoped [account, var:organizationId] is one counter per account and organization. Each counter is a scope key. In the web UI: Of, on the Plan page.

Self-metrics¶

Tapyr's metrics about itself, prefixed tapyr_, on /metrics/self: requests, retries, quotas, intervals, runs, circuits and outputs. They never carry identifiers such as serials or organization IDs. See Self-observability.

Series¶

One metric with one set of label values, such as the clients of one access point. Backends count series, and hosted ones bill by them; limits.maxSeries caps the series of an output. See Series and limits.

Simulator¶

An API served by Tapyr itself from the definition of a provider package, with its authentication, pagination and limits, and fault scenarios (429s, outages, slow responses): tapyr sim. It lets you try a configuration and its quota spend without an account.

Stale¶

A series that is no longer produced: it leaves the Prometheus exposition, and push outputs receive a stale marker. A series goes stale when the API no longer returns it, or when its job keeps failing for longer than staleAfter (3 times the effective interval by default).

State store¶

The SQLite database of tapyr run (settings.state.path): the spend of every quota window, the inventories, the run history, the tokens and the series of every job instance, so that a restart forgets nothing. See State.

Stretch¶

The factor by which the planner lengthens the interval of a job to fit a quota: x2 runs it half as often. Jobs of a priority are stretched together, up to 12 times (settings.budget.maxStretch), before the next priority up. In the web UI: Slowed down, on the Plan page.

Target¶

One device or endpoint of a multi-target account, such as one router of a fleet reached on its local API. Every job of the account runs once per target, and a target can override the params, secrets and labels of the account.

Unexpected 429¶

A 429 Too Many Requests that no declared limit predicted: the provider definition misses a limit, or another client shares it. Counted in tapyr_budget_unexpected_429_total; Quota problems, in the troubleshooting guide, says what to check.

Window¶

The period a quota counts over. A calendar window resets at a fixed time (every day at 00:00 UTC, the first of every month); a rolling window counts the last hour or the last day at any moment. The state store keeps what was spent, so a restart does not start the window again.
